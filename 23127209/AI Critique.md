During this assignment, the AI was useful for generating ideas and speeding up the testing process, but its responses were not always complete or reliable. One issue was that it did not include a test case for invalid input when the system was in the unlocked state. This scenario was not explicitly described in the requirements, so the AI overlooked it, while an experienced tester could identify it based on practical knowledge. The AI also missed a test case to verify that the counter increased by exactly one. Although this behavior may seem obvious, testers still need to validate it to ensure the system works correctly. Another limitation was that the AI focused more on input conditions than on expected outputs, which caused it to overlook several important output-related test cases. After I asked it to review its work, it was able to identify and add some of the missing cases. I also noticed that the AI's answers were not always consistent. As we moved to later stages of the assignment, it introduced new information that should have appeared in earlier responses, meaning I had to go back and update previous work myself. In some cases, the AI even made assumptions about test cases before the analysis had reached that stage, or shifted its attention to unrelated features. From this assignment, I learned that AI is a valuable assistant, but it should not be treated as a replacement for human judgment. The best results come from working with AI critically—reviewing its suggestions, questioning its assumptions, and using testing experience to identify gaps that it may miss.
